# Illustration quality and detail

Use when creating, revising or selecting collectible-card artwork. These criteria guide decisions; technical checks do not constitute the user's aesthetic acceptance.

## A scene worth keeping

Plan the moment before its decoration: what is happening, what the person notices, how the person responds, and what the viewer discovers through the composition. A new dress, background or ornate frame alone does not establish a new visual idea. Keep a clear focal hierarchy and deliberate foreground/middle/background; detail should support the event rather than bury it in flowers, jewelry, bloom or particles.

Keep identity references separate from pose, expression and lighting references. A supplied front-facing portrait does not require every card to look at the viewer. Choose profile, three-quarter, rear, overhead, close or environmental views when they tell the scene better. A back view can remain recognizable through hair, horns, ears, clothing details and silhouette. Do not respond to excessive front portraits by making the entire set back views.

Make gaze and expression respond to an object, action or person in the scene. Concentration, quiet observation, relief, surprise, an open laugh and closed-eye contentment have different brow/eyelid/mouth/shoulder relationships. Preserve facial identity without freezing expression. An open mouth by itself does not prove emotion, and an eye mask requires expression through mouth, posture and hands.

## Believable anatomy and contacts

Inspect complete images before admiring polish. Follow torso/spine → shoulder line → neck → skull; check that a lowered or turned head has a plausible neck and shoulder relationship. An extreme tilt or twist cannot be excused by pretty hair. Natural head tilt remains available when the posture supports it.

For visible legs, follow hip → thigh → knee → calf → ankle → shoe, including hidden segments and crossing order. Check each leg separately, pelvic support and the actual weight-bearing foot. Do not infer correct topology from two attractive shoes. Crossed legs, dancing feet and suspended limbs are not automatically defects; their chains, support and action must read clearly. Do not invent a brick or platform to justify a bad pose.

Check hands against objects: a finger must wrap the actual handle, a bow must be held and meet strings, a hand must touch the depicted glass/rail, and a seat must support the pelvis. If an essential hand is occluded beyond verification, change the camera or gesture rather than calling the contact passed. Revise the smallest actual cause; when the composition itself is the cause, make a new composition instead of accumulating patches.

## Frames, foil and depth serve this picture

Inspect the product's best existing templates as visual references. Borrow their design language, hierarchy and material logic, then draw a frame suited to this image: motif, corner mass, open edges, material, highlights and palette. Merely replacing a badge or recoloring one universal rectangle is not a designed collection. Inspect at thumbnail and normal card size; keep faces, hands, hair contours and action props readable.

Read what makes the reference delicate before adding ornament. In Luma's LM-B reference, fine antique silver scrolls, small individually drawn leaves and muted blue rose petals belong to the painted scene. Replacing them with chunky polished jewelry, bright gem beads or rope-like metal changes the aesthetic even if the output is technically detailed. Preserve each pack's own botanical, neural or orbital structure rather than copying that reference's flowers into every pack. Compare the reference and the framed real illustration side by side at the same displayed width.

When the product organizes cards into packs, establish a recognizable frame family for each actual published pack: distinct silhouette, engraving rhythm, primary emblem and material palette. Use the product's authoritative pack-routing function rather than a second guessed character-to-pack table. Within that family, adapt corner weight, open rails, color accents and foil regions to each picture; preserve reviewed clearances around heads, hands and props. When the user explicitly chooses individual scene-matched frames, let the pictured scene determine the ornament instead of forcing the shared pack texture onto every front; retain the real back and routing. A pack may include several characters without losing its shared frame identity. Respect existing authored craft and the delivery rights of retired packs; changing presentation must not change issuance, ownership, rarity or collection counts.

For paired front/back designs, actually view the latest approved card backs before proposing the front frame. Match their specific motifs and material behavior; a pack title alone is not an art reference. In the current Luma set, the sources are neural crystal/circuit/opals, ivory nacre rose/champagne gold/pink gem, cobalt rose/sapphire/silver-gold Gothic tracery, and amethyst lunar astrolabe/lavender iris. Do not substitute a travel compass or ice-blue snow frame. Show the back beside the framed character art to assess whether they belong together.

Keep the illustration dominant. Prefer slender rails and a few small, asymmetric accents, with distinct circuit, botanical, architectural and orbital structures instead of four similarly ornate corner-heavy rectangles. Large flowers, corner gems, crowns and medallions can overwhelm a small card even when finely rendered. For current Luma fronts, start with a fine main edge around20-40px on a1024px-wide card and judge complete corner ornaments at the actual displayed size. These are composition targets, not permission to clip a flower in half. Inspect the framed artwork at the real viewport size, not only the isolated frame. Bound foil and alpha to actual ornament; force the portrait window clear and keep relief restrained. Validate alpha from decoded RGBA pixels: image metadata or statistics that ignore a crop do not prove a clean central window.

Do not obtain a slender frame by shrinking complete flowers into tiny corner crops and repeating arbitrary thin samples from its rails. That can erase the drawing and break continuous vines or orbital arcs. Prefer a complete composition at the intended aspect; allow reviewed local ornaments to exceed a numeric guide when the actual illustration remains clear. Size a transparent-window guard from decoded solid ornament bounds, never cut petals or leaves merely to pass a fixed inset. Static front lighting should preserve painted material colors; angle-dependent foil may become more visible as the card turns without bleaching silver or tinting every flower cyan.

Use angle-dependent foil in deliberate regions such as metal engraving, glass, water, cloth accents or frame ornament. Keep skin/face and other quiet focal areas clean. All-new-card foil is a current Luma requirement, not a universal requirement for every product. Reduced-motion/static modes must retain a coherent designed card without compulsory animation.

Distinguish painted volume, physical card thickness, raised frame relief, independent subject/scene layers and actual sculpted geometry. Name the achieved form accurately. Rotation alone is not content depth. For the current Luma request every new card needs foil and visible 3D depth: genuine front/back/side geometry plus raised ornaments can serve frame relief; when the chosen design includes subject/environment parallax, use correctly registered real layers for it. Do not fabricate segmentation, empty depth assets, checkerboard Alpha or character animation. Inspect front, left/right tilt and resize for clipping, exposed gaps, double imagery and washed-out foil.

For a complete premium upgrade, audit every retained exact edition, including legacy viewer URLs and same-art revisions. Check foil and depth separately: an existing foil shader can still have a flat front, while a layered model can lack foil. Preserve successful original craft and add only the missing treatment. A regional RGB-derived micro-normal with independent directional light can provide restrained metal-surface relief; describe it as surface lighting, never as a new character mesh or geometric displacement. A thin print may instead need actual exterior bevel facets and a raised lip, with colors chosen from its picture. Keep these physical edges outside the complete original image.

Verify representative weak cases in the actual GPU viewer, including the smallest authored mask, a landscape front and a procedural-foil card. Compare a quiet front with oblique views and the static original; inspect the original back and reduced-motion final pose too. Source flags, nonzero masks and shader unit tests support this review but do not replace it. Record which cards were rendered and which received exact binding or pure checks; do not turn representative checks into a claim that every edition was visually inspected.

## Review and retain honestly

Use one bounded production review of the final source and actual rendered card. Record concrete findings, meaningful deviations from the brief, and any corrected failure. A prompt, PNG dimensions, unique hash, shader-ready flag or producer's "no defects" sentence cannot prove anatomy or beauty. Recheck only new changes, a real failure or user feedback; do not create an endless self-approval loop.

When selecting older art, compare its actual identity, composition, expression, anatomy, finish and usefulness in the set against the current direction. Do not retain it because of an old "completed" tag, rarity or sunk production effort. Where the user chooses errata retention, keep the old exact edition's runtime image, viewer, comments and holdings; classify it through the supported source pipeline and register the replacement separately. Follow the current visibility contract: Luma conceals errata in public pools, gives every edition an anonymous master-collection slot before acquisition, and reveals owned editions without a dedicated errata section. Concealment must not erase existing ownership or make an acquired edition inaccessible.

For Luma's current ten-card target, count independent active standard fronts per character, excluding errata, protected role-page art, history and same-art parallel finishes. Recompute after old-card selection and fill actual deficits with new designs. A changed card costume is illustration content, not authorization to replace the character's runtime skin.
